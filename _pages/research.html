▸ CFD-Based Control of Convection.

Employing the control law for regulating convection can provide an optimal system for many sophisticated applications, such as MEMS fabrication, electronic component cooling, and drug manufacturing facilities. A computational approach is always advantageous in this regard for affordable trial and error. A more advanced version of this can be established by creating a digital twin for the application. Other improvements include the use of nonlinear controllers for the system, inserting multiple sensors in the domain, and acquiring precise measurements. Another aspect involves quantifying the uncertainty and using it during feedback. This would require advanced data analysis in real-time and statistical methods to capture the system's dynamics and enable precise control.

A related published work (Deb et al., 2023†) only touches upon the basic aspects. This can be leveraged for many advanced applications with the aforementioned improvements.

▸ Complex System Optimization – Analytical and Data-Driven Approach

Generally, a complex system involves many decisions or design variables, often with multiple objectives. It includes improving the performance, efficiency, or robustness of a system in the face of highly non-linear relations, high dimensionality, and interdependence – features frequently encountered in real-world engineering problems. The analytical approaches have defined mathematical models or a closed-form solution. The objective function is smooth and differentiable, where gradient-based algorithms offer a viable solution in most convex optimization cases. The method also integrates data-driven surrogates with lower computational costs. However, it is also evident that the objective functions are sometimes not analytic or do not have a defined mathematical form. Moreover, highly non-convex optimization is critical, where many data-driven algorithms (ML, DL) and Bayesian optimizations are necessary. Metaheuristic-based algorithms (such as genetic, differential evolution, particle swarm, etc.) are helpful for the non-convex cases.
